# Code of Conduct

linearly exists so that anyone in the world can learn serious mathematics for free. The people
who build it hold themselves to the standard the course sets for its writing: clear, honest,
and kind.

## The rules

- **Be kind.** Assume good faith. Many contributors are students, many are not native English
  speakers, and for some this is the first project they have ever contributed to. Treat every
  question as a reasonable one.
- **Be precise.** Criticize work, never people. "This proof skips the rank-deficient case" is
  useful; "you clearly don't understand rank" is not, and it is banned twice over, since we
  don't say "clearly" here.
- **Settle math with math.** Disagreements about mathematics are settled by proof or
  computation, not by authority or volume.
- **Credit honestly.** Never present someone else's work, text, or images as your own, and
  never contribute material you don't have the right to give.
- **No harassment of any kind.** Personal attacks, discrimination, sexualized language,
  doxxing, and sustained hostility have no place here, in issues, PRs, or anywhere the project
  lives.

## Enforcement

Violations can be reported privately to the maintainers through GitHub: open a security
advisory on this repository (Security → Advisories → "Report a vulnerability", which GitHub
keeps private) or use GitHub's report mechanisms on the offending content. Reports are read
by a human, kept confidential, and acted on.
Consequences scale from a conversation to a permanent ban, at the maintainers' judgment.

Maintainers hold themselves to this document more strictly than they hold anyone else.
